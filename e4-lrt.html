4.1 Likelihood ratio test#

In 9 of 9 exams1–1.5 points35 min

The idea in one minute#

You get two possible stories for a measurement \(x\), each with its own PDF (the curve that says which values of \(x\) are likely): \(\mathcal H_0\) "nothing is there" and \(\mathcal H_1\) "something is there". You measured one \(x\). Ask each story: "how likely was this \(x\) under you?" — the height of its PDF at \(x\). If the \(\mathcal H_1\) curve is much higher at your \(x\), say \(\mathcal H_1\).

So the test is a ratio of heights compared with a number \(\lambda\) — the bar ("how convinced do I need to be"). The exam gives this bar different clothes: \(\eta\) (June 2026), \(\gamma\) (Nov 2022), or just hands it to you (\(\lambda=0.2\)):

\[ L(x)=\frac{p(x\mid\mathcal H_1)}{p(x\mid\mathcal H_0)}\ \underset{\mathcal H_0}{\overset{\mathcal H_1}{\gtrless}}\ \lambda \qquad\text{(bigger than }\lambda\text{ → say }\mathcal H_1\text{, smaller → say }\mathcal H_0). \]

Who is who (the letters the exam mixes up):

symbol lives on what it is
\(p(x\mid\mathcal H_1),\ p(x\mid\mathcal H_0)\) the two curves the PDF heights
\(L(x)\) — the ratio of the two heights (only the PDFs)
\(\lambda\) (= exam's \(\eta\)) the ratio the bar the ratio must beat
\(\gamma\) the \(x\) axis the cut you get when you solve \(L(x)>\lambda\) for \(x\)

Rule of thumb: the number they give you is the bar; the number they ask you to find is the cut. \(\lambda\) is always just a number, even with 1000 samples; \(L\) is always a function; \(\gamma\) is a number only when the \(N\)-dimensional region collapses to one axis (Nov 2022 4c: "show that the test takes the form \(\sum_n|x_n|\gtrless\gamma'\)").

After a bit of algebra this always becomes a cut on the \(x\) axis. Two mistakes are possible, and the threshold trades one for the other:

what happened name area under...
\(P_{FA}\) \(\mathcal H_0\) true, you said \(\mathcal H_1\) false alarm the \(\mathcal H_0\) curve, in the "decide \(\mathcal H_1\)" zone
\(P_M\) \(\mathcal H_1\) true, you said \(\mathcal H_0\) miss the \(\mathcal H_1\) curve, in the "decide \(\mathcal H_0\)" zone
\(P_D=1-P_M\) \(\mathcal H_1\) true, you said \(\mathcal H_1\) detection the \(\mathcal H_1\) curve, in the "decide \(\mathcal H_1\)" zone

Drag the threshold. Watch the blue area (\(P_{FA}\)) and the red area (\(P_M\)): you can never shrink both at once.

Now switch to the Nov 2022 tab. There the \(\mathcal H_1\) curve is the one that is tall near 0, so small values point to \(\mathcal H_1\): the rule becomes "\(y<\gamma\)" and the false alarm area sits on the left. This is the trap the exam loves.

How to fight this section with the sheet#

The exam gives two PDFs and says "likelihood ratio test", "binary hypothesis", "when accept \(H_1\)", threshold \(\lambda/\gamma/\eta\), "\(P_{FA}\)"/"\(P_D\)" → you open box 4A on your A4 sheet. Four numbered steps, then one worked line per exam that has ever appeared, then a Probabilities block for the areas. The multiple choice at the end of the question is box 4C.

Box 4A, exactly as it is printed on the sheet

IF YOU SEE two pdfs (one for \(H_0\), one for \(H_1\)) + "binary hypothesis"/"hypothesis testing", "likelihood ratio test", "when accept \(H_1\)", threshold \(\lambda,\gamma,\eta\), "\(P_{FA}\)"/"\(P_D\)"

  1. \(L(x)=\dfrac{p(x\mid H_1)}{p(x\mid H_0)}\). Cancel what appears on top and bottom. \(N\) samples → multiply them (\(e^ae^b=e^{a+b}\)).
  2. Decide \(H_1\) if \(L\gt\) threshold. Take \(\ln\) of both sides.
  3. Get \(x\) alone. Multiplying or dividing by a negative number flips \(\gt\) into \(\lt\).
  4. Check the side: put a small and a large \(x\) into \(L\). Where \(L\) comes out bigger than the threshold, that side is "decide \(H_1\)".

Apr25 \(\frac{6x^{11}}{3x^5}=2x^6\gt0.2\) → \(x^6\gt0.1\) → \(x\gt0.1^{1/6}=0.68\).

Jun26 Rayleigh \(\frac x{\sigma^2}e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}\) (\(\sigma_1\gt\sigma_0\)): the \(x\) in front cancels → accept \(H_1\) if \(x\gt\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_0^2\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_1^2-\sigma_0^2}\,\ln\!\big(\eta\frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_0^2}\big)}\). Bigger gap between \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1\) → the pdfs overlap less → more \(P_D\) for the same \(P_{FA}\) (better ROC).

Apr24 Gaussian \(\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-y^2/2}\) vs exponential \(\frac14e^{-y/2}\), \(\gamma=1\): take \(\ln\) → \(-0.467+\frac{y^2}2-\frac y2\gt0\) → \(y^2-y-0.934\gt0\) → root \(y=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+3.74}}2=1.59\) → accept \(H_1\) if \(y\gt1.59\). \(P_{FA}=Q(1.59)=0.056\), \(P_D=\frac12e^{-0.795}=0.23\).

Nov22 \(\frac12e^{-\lvert x\rvert}\) vs \(e^{-2\lvert x\rvert}\), \(N\) samples: \(L=2^Ne^{-\sum\lvert x_n\rvert}\) → accept \(H_1\) if \(\sum\lvert x_n\rvert\lt N\ln2-\ln\lambda\).

Probabilities. \(P_{FA}\) = area under the \(H_0\) pdf where you decide \(H_1\). \(P_D\) = area under the \(H_1\) pdf in the same place.
Tails: Gaussian \(N(0,1)\): \(P(x\gt t)=Q(t)\) (table) · exponential \(ce^{-cx}\): \(P(x\gt t)=e^{-ct}\) · Rayleigh: \(P(x\gt t)=e^{-t^2/2\sigma^2}\) · two tails \(\lvert x\rvert\gt t\) → double.
"Threshold for \(P_{FA}=0.1\)": set the \(H_0\) area \(=0.1\), solve for \(t\).

Nov22: \(1-e^{-t}=0.1\) → \(t=-\ln0.9\); then \(P_D=1-e^{-2t}=0.19\).

"Plot / indicate \(P_{FA}\) and \(P_M\)": draw both pdfs and a vertical line at the threshold. Shade the \(H_0\) curve on the "decide \(H_1\)" side → \(P_{FA}\). Shade the \(H_1\) curve on the "decide \(H_0\)" side → \(P_M\). (Nov22: \(H_1\) when \(y\lt1\), so \(P_{FA}\) = \(H_0\) area left of 1, \(P_M\) = \(H_1\) area right of 1.)

Gaussian with any \(\sigma\). \(p(x)=\dfrac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}\) (mean 0). Two zero-mean Gaussians, \(\sigma_1\) vs \(\sigma_0\) (pe21-4b): \(\ln L=N\ln\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_1}+\frac12\big(\frac1{\sigma_0^2}-\frac1{\sigma_1^2}\big)\sum_n x^2[n]\) → decide \(H_1\) when \(\sum_n x^2[n]\) is big = energy detector (bigger variance = more energy).
erf table (printed in the question — pe21-4a): the printed CDF \(\frac12\big[1+\operatorname{erf}\frac{t-\mu}{\sigma\sqrt2}\big]\) is \(P(x\lt t)\); the test needs \(P(x\gt t)=1-\text{CDF}=\frac12\big[1-\operatorname{erf}\frac{z}{\sqrt2}\big]\), \(z=\frac{t-\mu}{\sigma}\). The table lists \(\operatorname{erf}(x/\sqrt2)\) at \(x=-3..3\): read it at \(x=z\), value at \(-z\) = minus the value at \(z\).
Matched filter (jan22-4a): correlate the data with the known signal \(s[n]\) (FIR filter \(h[n]=\) the time-reversed \(s\)) → biggest output SNR; "only for uncorrelated noise" is false.

The algebra you need mid-step (\(\ln\) rules, \(e\) rules, quadratics, \(Q\) numbers) is in the Calculations strip at the bottom of the sheet.

1 · April 2025 · 4a — the sheet's first example#

Final exam, 13 April 2025 · 4a · 0.5 p

You are conducting a likelihood ratio test using a single datapoint \(x\), \(0 < x < 1\) whereby \(p(x) = k_1 \theta x^{k_2 \theta - 1}\). Two hypothesis can be distinguished

\[\mathcal{H}_0 : \theta = 3, \qquad \mathcal{H}_1 : \theta = 6.\]

If \(\lambda = 0.2\), \(k_1 = 1\), and \(k_2 = 2\), what is the value for \(x\) for which \(\mathcal{H}_1\) should be accepted?

In the cheat sheet: box 4A, the four steps (the Apr25 line runs them on these numbers)

Sheet step 1 · build the two PDFs (the numbers are given). \(p(x)=\theta x^{2\theta-1}\) → \(H_0\): \(3x^5\), \(H_1\): \(6x^{11}\).

Sheet step 2 · step 1 of the box: the ratio, cancel. "\(L(x)=\frac{p(x\mid H_1)}{p(x\mid H_0)}\). Cancel what appears on top and bottom." \(\frac{6x^{11}}{3x^5}=2x^6\) — the sheet's line: "\(\frac{6x^{11}}{3x^5}=2x^6\gt0.2\)".

Sheet step 3 · step 3: get \(x\) alone. \(x^6>0.1\) → \(x>0.1^{1/6}=0.68\) (a root is increasing → no flip). The sheet's line ends "\(x\gt0.1^{1/6}=0.68\)".

Sheet step 4 · step 4: check the side. "put a small and a large \(x\) into \(L\)": at \(x=0.9\), \(L=2(0.9)^6\approx1.06>0.2\) ✓ decide \(H_1\); at \(x=0.1\), \(L\approx0\) → decide \(H_0\). So big \(x\) = \(H_1\), and the problem's own range \(0<x<1\) caps it.

Sheet check · the box's step 4. The box's own test: "put a small and a large \(x\) into \(L\)" — big \(x\) gives \(L>0.2\) ✓, small \(x\) gives \(L\approx0\). That is how you fix the side for any threshold.

Answer

Accept \(\mathcal H_1\) for \(x>0.1^{1/6}\approx0.68\) (i.e. \(0.68<x<1\)).

2 · November 2022 · 4c–4e — \(N\) samples, the flip, and the areas#

Final exam, 7 November 2022 · 4c (0.5 p), 4d (0.25 p), 4e (0.5 p)

Under the two hypotheses, a single observation \(x\) admits a zero mean Laplacian distribution, but with different variances:

\[\mathcal{H}_0 : p(x; \mathcal{H}_0) = \frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathcal{H}_1 : p(x; \mathcal{H}_1) = e^{-2|x|}.\]

Each observation \(x_n\) of the vector \(\mathbf{x}\) (length \(N\)) is independent and identically distributed.

4c Show that the likelihood ratio test takes the form \(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} |x_n| \underset{\mathcal{H}_1}{\overset{\mathcal{H}_0}{\gtrless}} \gamma'\) and determine \(\gamma'\).

4d For \(N = 1\), with \(Y=|X|\): \(p(y; \mathcal{H}_0) = e^{-y}\), \(p(y; \mathcal{H}_1) = 2e^{-2y}\). Indicate \(P_{FA}\) and \(P_M\) for \(\gamma' = 1\) in the plot.

4e For a \(P_{FA} = 0.1\) calculate the threshold \(\gamma'\), and the probability of detection \(P_D\) for it.

In the cheat sheet: box 4A — the Nov22 lines answer 4c, 4d and 4e

Sheet step 1 · 4c = the box's Nov22 line. "\(\frac12e^{-\lvert x\rvert}\) vs \(e^{-2\lvert x\rvert}\), \(N\) samples: \(L=2^Ne^{-\sum\lvert x_n\rvert}\) → accept \(H_1\) if \(\sum\lvert x_n\rvert\lt N\ln2-\ln\lambda\)." That is the requested form with \(\gamma'=N\ln2-\ln\lambda\). (One sample: \(\frac{e^{-2|x|}}{\frac12 e^{-|x|}}=2e^{-|x|}\); \(N\) independent samples → step 1's "N samples → multiply them" → \(2^Ne^{-\sum|x_n|}\); take \(\ln\) (step 2), then step 3 flips the inequality when you multiply by \(-1\): \(\sum|x_n|<N\ln2-\ln\lambda\).)

Sheet step 2 · 4d = the box's "Plot / indicate" line. "draw both pdfs and a vertical line at the threshold. Shade the \(H_0\) curve on the 'decide \(H_1\)" side → \(P_{FA}\). Shade the \(H_1\) curve on the 'decide \(H_0\)" side → \(P_M\). (Nov22: \(H_1\) when \(y\lt1\), so \(P_{FA}\) = \(H_0\) area left of 1, \(P_M\) = \(H_1\) area right of 1.)" The sheet even names which side — the rule points left because \(H_1\) is the narrow curve.

Sheet step 3 · 4e = the box's threshold line. "'Threshold for \(P_{FA}=0.1\)': set the \(H_0\) area \(=0.1\), solve for \(t\)." With the exponential tail formula ("exponential \(ce^{-cx}\): \(P(x>t)=e^{-ct}\)", here \(c=1\), and \(P_{FA}\) is the left area \(1-e^{-t}\)): \(1-e^{-t}=0.1\) → \(t=-\ln0.9\approx0.105\).

Sheet step 4 · \(P_D\) = the same line's tail. "then \(P_D=1-e^{-2t}=0.19\)" — the same tail recipe with the \(H_1\) rate \(c=2\): \(1-e^{-2(0.105)}=0.19\).

Sheet check · the box's three lines. The Probabilities block solves all three sub-questions: tails for \(P_{FA}\), the same place under \(H_1\) for \(P_D\), and "set the \(H_0\) area \(=0.1\), solve for \(t\)" for the threshold. The numbers come out of those lines.

Answer

4c: \(\sum_n|x_n|\ \underset{\mathcal H_1}{\overset{\mathcal H_0}{\gtrless}}\ \gamma'=N\ln2-\ln\lambda\) (small sum → \(H_1\)). 4d: \(P_{FA}\) = \(e^{-y}\) area left of 1, \(P_M\) = \(2e^{-2y}\) area right of 1. 4e: \(\gamma'=-\ln0.9\approx0.105\), \(P_D=1-e^{-2\gamma'}=0.19\).

3 · June 2026 · 4b — Rayleigh, straight from the box#

Resit Q4, June 2026 · 4b · 1 p

A wireless sensor measures the received signal envelope \(X\). Under \(H_0\) (no device present), \(X \sim \text{Rayleigh}(\sigma_0)\). Under \(H_1\) (device present), \(X \sim \text{Rayleigh}(\sigma_1)\), with \(\sigma_1 > \sigma_0 > 0\), both known. The Rayleigh pdf is \(p(x \mid \sigma) = \frac{x}{\sigma^2} \exp\left(-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\), \(x \geq 0\).

  • Construct the likelihood-ratio test for a threshold \(\eta\).
  • Discuss how the separation between \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_1\) affects detection performance.

In the cheat sheet: box 4A, the four steps (the Jun26 Rayleigh line runs them on this pdf)

Sheet step 1 · build the ratio, cancel what cancels. Box step 1: "Cancel what appears on top and bottom" — the \(\frac x{\sigma^2}\) in both pdfs cancels and you are left with \(L=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{\sigma_1^2}\,e^{\frac{x^2}2\left(\frac1{\sigma_0^2}-\frac1{\sigma_1^2}\right)}\). (The sheet's Rayleigh line says it in words: "the \(x\) in front cancels".)

Sheet step 2 · solve for \(x\) = the printed formula. "accept \(H_1\) if \(x\gt\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_0^2\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_1^2-\sigma_0^2}\,\ln\!\big(\eta\frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_0^2}\big)}\)." Take \(\ln\) (step 2), isolate \(x\) (step 3 — no flip because \(\sigma_1>\sigma_0\) makes the bracket positive), done.

Sheet step 3 · the discussion = the line's second sentence. "Bigger gap between \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1\) → the pdfs overlap less → more \(P_D\) for the same \(P_{FA}\) (better ROC)." That is the whole discussion bullet.

Sheet check · the box's rule. The discussion line is a statement about any pair of \(\sigma\)'s; write it in words and you have the point, numbers or not.

Answer

Decide \(H_1\) if \(x>\gamma=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\sigma_0^2\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_1^2-\sigma_0^2}\,\ln\!\Big(\eta\,\dfrac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_0^2}\Big)}\). Further apart \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1\) → less overlap → higher \(P_D\) at the same \(P_{FA}\) (better ROC); \(\sigma_1\to\sigma_0\) → no better than guessing.

4 · April 2024 · 4b — Gaussian vs exponential, \(\gamma=1\)#

Final exam, April 2024 · 4b · 1.5 p

\[\mathcal{H}_0: p(y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{y^2}{2}}\qquad \mathcal{H}_1: p(y) = \frac{1}{4} e^{-\frac{y}{2}},\qquad y > 0.\]

Sketch the likelihoods. For a threshold \(\gamma = 1\), indicate \(P_{FA}\) and \(P_M\), and calculate \(P_D\) and \(P_{FA}\). Use \(Q(x) = \int_x^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{u^2}{2}} du\). No need for exact numerical values.

In the cheat sheet: box 4A, the four steps (the Apr24 line runs them end to end)

Sheet step 1 · the bar is on the ratio. \(\gamma=1\) means \(L>1\): "decide the story whose curve is higher". (Trap: it is not a value of \(y\).)

Sheet step 2 · the printed computation. "\(L=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}4e^{y^2/2-y/2}\gt1\); take \(\ln\): \(-0.467+\frac{y^2}2-\frac y2\gt0\) → \(y^2-y-0.934\gt0\) → root \(y=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+3.74}}2=1.59\) → accept \(H_1\) if \(y\gt1.59\)." Steps 2–3 of the box: \(\ln\) both sides, get \(y\) alone (quadratic formula from the Calculations strip).

Sheet step 3 · the areas = the Probabilities block. "\(P_{FA}=Q(1.59)=0.056\)" (Gaussian tail: "Gaussian \(N(0,1)\): \(P(x\gt t)=Q(t)\)"), "\(P_D=\int_{1.59}^\infty\frac14e^{-y/2}dy=\frac12e^{-0.795}=0.23\)" (exponential tail \(e^{-ct}\) with \(c=\frac12\)).

Sheet step 4 · the shading. The "Plot / indicate" line: decide \(H_1\) for \(y>1.59\) → shade the \(H_0\) curve to the right of 1.59 (\(P_{FA}\)), the \(H_1\) curve to the left (\(P_M\)).

Sheet check · the tails recipe. Gaussian tail \(=Q(t)\), exponential tail \(=e^{-ct}\) — those two lines produce \(P_{FA}=Q(1.59)\) and \(P_D=\frac12e^{-0.795}\) whatever threshold you found. The exam says "no exact numbers needed": the \(Q\) form is the answer.

Answer

Decide \(\mathcal H_1\) for \(y>1.59\). \(P_{FA}=Q(1.59)\approx0.056\), \(P_D=\frac12 e^{-0.795}\approx0.23\), \(P_M\approx0.27\) (as printed; the densities are not normalised on \(y>0\), so \(P_D+P_M=\frac12\)).

5 · The theory questions — box 4C#

The MC is not about calculating: the same dozen sentences come back with one word swapped. Who puts the number \(\lambda\) in place? Neyman–Pearson: fix the false alarms you can tolerate, maximise \(P_D\). Bayes (4.2): price each mistake + prior, minimise the average cost. Either way you compare the ratio with a number.

Box 4C, exactly as it is printed on the sheet

IF YOU SEE "which of the following statements" (in Part 4) about hypothesis testing, \(P_{FA}\), errors

Same swap test as 2D — the verb is the question: risk min · \(P_D\) max · \(P_e\) min; \(\eta\) goes up with \(P(H_0)\), down with \(P(H_1)\).

The fact (true) The lie they print (false)
Bayes detection minimises the Bayes risk "maximises"
\(C_{01}=C_{10}\), \(C_{00}=C_{11}=0\) → minimises total error \(P_e\) (scaling all costs leaves \(\eta\))
Equal priors + equal costs → maximum-likelihood rule —
Higher \(P(H_0)\) raises \(\eta\) → harder to pick \(H_1\) "higher \(P(H_1)\) raises \(\eta\)" (lowers — Jun26 c)
Neyman–Pearson: fix \(P_{FA}\), maximise \(P_D\) "minimise \(P_D\)" · "fix \(P_D\), max \(P_{FA}\)"
NP's solution is the LRT; the LRT itself needs no priors "the LRT needs \(P_0,P_1\)"
\(\ln\) of the ratio never changes the decisions "changes the decisions"
Errors are unavoidable: the pdfs overlap "a threshold kills both errors"
ROC: \(P_D\) up, \(P_{FA}\) right; diagonal = guessing; better = top-left "below diagonal = wrong \(\sigma\)" (flipped)
miss = false negative = type II · false alarm = false positive = type I (Nov22 a) labels swapped

Matched filter & energy-detector sentences → the last blocks of 4A (time-reversed \(s\), "only for uncorrelated noise" false, \(\sum x^2\) big, narrow \(H_1\) → sum small).

Trap (Apr25-4b): "which statement is FALSE" where all three look true → as printed all three are true (official: no false one). Check the verbs: risk min · \(P_D\) max · \(P_e\) min.

Sheet step 1 · the error-table question (Nov 2022 4a). The last table row names the cells: "miss = false negative = type II · false alarm = false positive = type I". "Positive" means "we said \(H_1\)". Match the shuffled cells to the row.

Sheet step 2 · the NP sentence (Nov 2022 4b, Apr 2024 4a): read the verb. Fact: "Neyman–Pearson: fix \(P_{FA}\), maximise \(P_D\)". "Minimise \(P_D\)" is the printed lie. A rephrasing ("among all tests with \(P_{FA}=\alpha\), the one with the highest detection rate…") changes nothing: same idea, same answer.

Sheet step 3 · the priors sentence (Jun 2026 4c): the lie is printed with the exam tag. "Higher \(P(H_0)\) raises \(\eta\) → harder to pick \(H_1\)" is the fact; "higher \(P(H_1)\) raises \(\eta\)" is the lie (it lowers it).

Sheet step 4 · the ROC sentence and its lie. Fact: "\(P_D\) up, \(P_{FA}\) right; diagonal = guessing; better = top-left". The false twin blames a wrong \(\sigma\) for a curve below the diagonal — a wrong \(\sigma\) only flattens it; below means flipped decisions.

Sheet step 5 · the matched filter (jan22-4a) and the energy detector (pe21-4b). These live in 4A's last blocks, not in the table: "correlate the data with the known signal \(s[n]\) (FIR filter \(h[n]=\) the time-reversed \(s\)) → biggest output SNR; "only for uncorrelated noise" is false", and "decide \(H_1\) when \(\sum_n x^2[n]\) is big = energy detector".

Sheet check · the trap line. Apr25-4b asks "which statement is FALSE" and, as printed, all three are true — the box says so explicitly. Check the three verbs before choosing: risk min, \(P_D\) max, \(P_e\) min.

Fresh wording, never printed in any exam — run the swap test:

1 · "The Neyman–Pearson lemma provides, among all tests with a given false-alarm rate, the one with the smallest miss probability." "Smallest miss" = "biggest \(P_D\)", same idea, no swapped verb → T.

2 · "Taking the logarithm of the likelihood ratio may change which observations are assigned to \(H_1\)." Swap: may change against the fact "never changes the decisions" → F.

3 · "A detector whose ROC runs below the diagonal should be re-fitted with a better noise model." Swap: the fact says below-diagonal = flipped decisions; a wrong noise model only flattens the curve → F.

Traps#

Where points are lost (each one is a line of the sheet you skipped)

  • Forgetting to flip when you multiply or divide by a negative (box step 3). Then the whole rule points the wrong way — the Nov22 case decides \(H_1\) for a small \(\sum|x_n|\).
  • Wrong curve for the area (Probabilities block): \(P_{FA}\) always uses \(p(x\mid H_0)\), \(P_D\) and \(P_M\) always use \(p(x\mid H_1)\).
  • Shading the wrong side: first decide where "decide \(H_1\)" is (box step 4: "put a small and a large \(x\) into \(L\)"), then shade.
  • Confusing the bar and the cut: the number they give (\(\lambda,\eta,\gamma\) on the ratio) is the bar; the number you solve for is the cut on \(x\).
  • \(N\) samples: the ratio is a product, its log a sum. Do not drop the \(N\) in front of constants (\(2^N\to N\ln2\)).
  • Using the CDF instead of the tail for \(P(x>t)\) (the erf-table block: the printed table is \(P(x<t)\), so \(P(x>t)=1-\text{CDF}\)).
  • In the MC: swapping "maximise"/"minimise" (box 4C row 6) or calling \(h[n]=s[n]\) the matched filter (box 4A: it is the time-reversed \(s\)).
