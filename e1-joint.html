1.2 Joint PDFs: find \(c\), marginal, conditional, independent#

In 4 of the last 4 exams0.3–0.5 points25 min

The idea in one minute#

You get a formula \(p_{X,Y}(x,y)\) that is non-zero only on some region of the \((x,y)\) plane. Think of it as a height above that region. Three facts:

  • The total volume under it is 1 (that is how you find \(c\)).
  • The marginal \(p_Y(y)\): stand at height \(y\), walk across the region horizontally, and add up the heights you pass: \(p_Y(y)=\int p(x,y)\,dx\) along that slice.
  • The only hard part is where the slice starts and ends. Read it off a drawing. Always draw the region.

How to fight this section with the sheet#

The exam gives a \(p(x,y)\) on a region and says "find \(c\)", "marginal", "conditional", "independent", "domain" → you open box 1A on your A4 sheet. Four bullets = the four questions, and each bullet ends with a worked line showing the moves on past numbers. There is also a "Doing the double integral" strip: the general calculation for the \(c\) question.

Box 1A, exactly as it is printed on the sheet

IF YOU SEE a joint PDF/PMF \(p(x,y)\) on a region + "find \(c\)" / "marginal" / "conditional" / "independent" / "domain"

First draw the region on \(x\)–\(y\) axes: every integral below takes its limits from that picture, never from the formula.

  • "Find \(c\)" → integrate \(p\) over the whole region, set the result \(=1\), solve for \(c\).
    Limits in 4 moves. (1) Choose the order \(dx\,dy\) = inner \(x\), outer \(y\) (the other order works too). (2) Freeze the outer letter at one value. (3) Inner limits: "from where to where can the inner letter move now?" — they may contain the outer letter (\(x\) from \(0\) to \(1-y\)). (4) Outer limits: the full range of the frozen letter, always plain numbers (\(y\) from \(0\) to \(1\)). Rectangle → both limits are numbers, order free. Triangle/wedge → one limit is an expression in the other letter. Limits can be \(\infty\) (Jun24): \(e^{-\infty}=0\).
    Apr25: \(cx+1\) on the triangle \(x,y\ge0\), \(x+y\lt1\) — the whole integral is worked out below.
  • "Marginal \(p_X(x)\)" → integrate the OTHER letter away: \(p_X(x)=\int p(x,y)\,dy\), inner limits as in move 3. Range of \(x\) = the region's shadow on the \(x\) axis; write it and "0 otherwise".
    Jun26: \(6x^2y\) on the square \(0\le x,y\le1\): \(p_X=\int_0^1 6x^2y\,dy=3x^2\) for \(0\le x\le1\). Same way \(p_Y=2y\).
  • "Conditional \(p(x\mid y)\)" → \(p(x,y)\) divided by \(p_Y(y)\); state the ranges (\(y\) over its shadow, \(x\) over the slice at that \(y\)).
    Apr24: \(p=2\) on \(0\le y\le x\le1\): \(p_Y=\int_y^1 2\,dx=2(1-y)\) → \(p(x\mid y)=\frac1{1-y}\) for \(y\le x\le1\).
  • "Independent?" → YES only if the region is a rectangle AND \(p(x,y)=p_X(x)\cdot p_Y(y)\).
    Jun26: square, and \(3x^2\cdot2y=6x^2y\) → independent. A triangle region → never independent.

Doing the double integral (the Apr25 one above, step by step). \(\int_0^1\!\int_0^{1-y}(cx+1)\,dx\,dy\):
(a) inner \(dx\) first — treat \(y\) as a plain number: \(\int_0^{1-y}(cx+1)dx=\big[\frac{cx^2}{2}+x\big]_0^{1-y}=\frac{c(1-y)^2}{2}+(1-y)\). Top limit minus bottom; at the bottom every \(x\) is 0. The answer still holding \(y\) is normal.
(b) now in \(y\) — expand first, \((1-y)^2=1-2y+y^2\): \(\int_0^1(1-y)dy=\big[y-\frac{y^2}{2}\big]_0^1=\frac12\) · \(\int_0^1\frac{(1-y)^2}{2}dy=\big[-\frac{(1-y)^3}{6}\big]_0^1=\frac16\) → total \(\frac c6+\frac12=1\) → \(c=3\).
Rules: \(\int x^n dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}\) · \(\int k\,dx=kx\) · \(\int(a-y)^n dy=-\frac{(a-y)^{n+1}}{n+1}\) · always top minus bottom · expand powers first.

One more sheet place: box 1E ("\(E[x]\) = the weighted average of the outcomes", "Discrete = the values can be listed") for the multiple-choice words at the bottom of this page.

1 · April 2025 · 1c — find \(c\) (the sheet works this one out for you)#

Final exam Q3, 13 April 2025 · 1c · 0.3 p

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be jointly continuous random variables with joint PDF given by:

\[p_{X,Y}(x,y) = \begin{cases} cx + 1 & x, y \geq 0, x + y < 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Note: For the conditions \(x, y \geq 0, x + y < 1\) to be satisfied, \(x < 1 - y\) and \(y < 1\), or equivalently \(y < 1 - x\) and \(x < 1\).

Provide only the final answer. The workout will not be checked.

Find the constant \(c\).

In the cheat sheet: box 1A, bullet 1 + the 'Doing the double integral' strip

Sheet step 1 · draw, then read the limits off the picture. The sheet's first line: "First draw the region … every integral below takes its limits from that picture." \(x,y\ge0\) and \(x+y<1\) = the triangle under the diagonal. The exam's own note gives the sheet's move 3: inner \(x\) from \(0\) to \(1-y\).

Sheet step 2 · the four moves. "(1) Choose the order \(dx\,dy\) = inner \(x\), outer \(y\). (2) Freeze the outer letter. (3) Inner limits … may contain the outer letter (\(x\) from \(0\) to \(1-y\)). (4) Outer limits … plain numbers (\(y\) from \(0\) to \(1\))." Triangle → "one limit is an expression in the other letter" — exactly what we have.

Sheet step 3 · inner integral (the strip's line (a)). \(\int_0^{1-y}(cx+1)dx=\big[\frac{cx^2}{2}+x\big]_0^{1-y}=\frac{c(1-y)^2}{2}+(1-y)\). The sheet warns: "The answer still holding \(y\) is normal."

Sheet step 4 · outer integral (the strip's line (b)). "expand first, \((1-y)^2=1-2y+y^2\)" → \(\int_0^1(1-y)dy=\frac12\) and \(\int_0^1\frac{(1-y)^2}{2}dy=\frac16\) → total \(\frac c6+\frac12=1\) → \(c=3\).

Sheet check · the box's own check. The strip's rule is that the whole integral has to come out \(=1\) — that equation is what gives \(c\). If you cannot write "\(\frac c6+\frac12=1\)", you stopped mid-strip.

Answer

\(c=3\)

2 · April 2024 · 1c — marginal + conditional, domains included#

Final exam Q3, April 2024 · 1c · 0.3 p

Random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) have joint PDF

\[p_{X,Y}(x,y) = \begin{cases} 2, & 0 \leq y \leq x \leq 1 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Find the marginal PDF of \(Y\) (\(p_Y(y)\)), and the conditional PDF \(p_{X|Y}(x|y)\). For both PDFs make sure to correctly indicate their domain.

Note: provide only the final answer. No passages will be checked.

In the cheat sheet: box 1A, bullets 3 and 4 (the Apr24 lines run the same bullets on other numbers)

Sheet step 1 · draw. \(0\le y\le x\le1\) = the triangle under the diagonal \(y=x\) (first tab of the widget). Freeze \(y\): walking right, the slice runs from the diagonal \(x=y\) to \(x=1\).

Sheet step 2 · marginal (bullet 3's example). "Apr24: \(p=2\) on \(0\le y\le x\le1\): \(p_Y=\int_y^1 2\,dx=2(1-y)\)." The sheet hands you the answer. Domain: bullet 2 — "write it and '0 otherwise'" → \(0\le y\le1\).

Sheet step 3 · conditional (bullet 4's rule + same example). "\(p(x,y)\) divided by \(p_Y(y)\); state the ranges (\(y\) over its shadow, \(x\) over the slice at that \(y\))." → \(\frac{2}{2(1-y)}=\frac1{1-y}\) for \(y\le x\le1\) (the slice), 0 otherwise.

Sheet check · the shape of the answer. Bullet 3 says "state the ranges (\(y\) over its shadow, \(x\) over the slice at that \(y\))" — your pair must carry both ranges whatever the numbers are, and that is where points get lost.

Answer

\(p_Y(y)=2(1-y)\) for \(0\le y\le1\) (0 otherwise); \(\;p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=\dfrac{1}{1-y}\) for \(y\le x\le1\) (0 otherwise).

3 · June 2026 · 1c — marginal + independence on a square#

Resit Q4, June 2026 · 1c · 0.5 p

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be jointly continuous random variables with a joint Probability Density Function (PDF) given by:

\[p_{X,Y}(x, y) = \begin{cases} 6x^2y, & 0 \leq x \leq 1 \text{ and } 0 \leq y \leq 1 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Please provide only the final answer. The workout will not be checked.

  1. Find the marginal PDF \(p_X(x)\) and indicate its domain. [0.25]
  2. Determine whether \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, and briefly justify why. [0.25]

In the cheat sheet: box 1A, bullets 3 and 5 (the Jun26 lines run the same bullets)

Sheet step 1 · the region. \(0\le x,y\le1\) = the unit square (third tab of the widget). The sheet's move 4: "Rectangle → both limits are numbers, order free" → every slice is 0 to 1.

Sheet step 2 · marginal (bullet 3's example). "Jun26: \(6x^2y\) on the square: \(p_X=\int_0^1 6x^2y\,dy=3x^2\) for \(0\le x\le1\)." The sheet hands you \(3x^2\). (If they ask \(p_Y\): "Same way \(p_Y=2y\).") Domain from bullet 2: the shadow, \(0\le x\le1\), "0 otherwise".

Sheet step 3 · independence (bullet 5's example). "YES only if the region is a rectangle AND \(p(x,y)=p_X(x)\cdot p_Y(y)\) … Jun26: square, and \(3x^2\cdot2y=6x^2y\) → independent." Both conditions printed → independent, and the justification is the multiplication \(3x^2\cdot 2y=6x^2y\) on a rectangle.

Sheet check · the box's two conditions. Bullet 5: "YES only if the region is a rectangle AND \(p(x,y)=p_X(x)\cdot p_Y(y)\)" — test both, in that order. The rectangle test alone already kills every triangle region.

Answer

  1. \(p_X(x)=3x^2\) for \(0\le x\le1\), 0 otherwise. 2. Independent: the support is a rectangle and \(p_{X,Y}=p_X\,p_Y=3x^2\cdot2y\).

4 · June 2024 · 1c — \(c\) with limits at infinity (MC)#

Resit June 2024 · 1c · 0.25 p

Random variables have joint PDF given by:

\[p_{X,Y}(x, y) = ce^{-x}e^{-y},\]

for \(0 \leq y \leq x < \infty\).

What is the correct values for the normalization constant \(c\)?

a) 1 b) 4 c) ½ d) 2

In the cheat sheet: box 1A, bullet 1 — the \(\infty\) line and the same four moves

Sheet step 1 · draw, freeze \(x\). \(0\le y\le x<\infty\): the wedge below the diagonal, open to the right. Move 3: inner \(y\) from \(0\) to \(x\) (an expression in the outer letter — "Triangle/wedge → one limit is an expression in the other letter"). Move 4: outer \(x\) from \(0\) to \(\infty\).

Sheet step 2 · inner. \(\int_0^x e^{-y}dy=1-e^{-x}\) (top minus bottom; the sheet's rule "always top minus bottom").

Sheet step 3 · outer, with the sheet's infinity line. "Limits can be \(\infty\) (Jun24): \(e^{-\infty}=0\)." \(\int_0^\infty e^{-x}(1-e^{-x})dx=\int_0^\infty e^{-x}dx-\int_0^\infty e^{-2x}dx=1-\frac12=\frac12\). (The \(e^{-\infty}=0\) is what makes both terms plain numbers.)

Sheet step 4 · solve. \(c\cdot\frac12=1\) → \(c=2\) → (d).

Answer

(d) \(c=2\)

5 · November 2022 · 1b — marginal from a table (sum, don't integrate)#

Final exam Q1, 7 November 2022 · 1b · 0.25 p

Consider two random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) with joint PMF \(p_{X,Y}(x,y)\) given by the following table. What is the marginal PMF of \(X\)?

\(Y = 0\) \(Y = 1\) \(Y = 2\)
\(X = 0\) ⅙ ¼ ⅛
\(X = 1\) ⅛ ⅙ ⅙
  • (a) \(p_X(0)=7/24\), \(p_X(1)=5/24\), 0 otherwise
  • (b) \(p_X(0)=13/24\), \(p_X(1)=11/24\), 0 otherwise
  • (c) \(p_X(0)=13/24\), \(p_X(1)=5/24\), \(p_X(2)=11/24\), 0 otherwise
  • (d) \(p_X(0)=7/24\), \(p_X(1)=5/12\), \(p_X(2)=7/24\), 0 otherwise

In the cheat sheet: box 1A, bullet 3 — same rule, table version

Sheet step 1 · the rule. "Marginal \(p_X(x)\) → integrate the OTHER letter away." With a table there is nothing to integrate: sum the other letter away = add each row across. (Box 1E: "Discrete = the values can be listed" — a table is the discrete twin of a joint PDF.)

Sheet step 2 · common denominator, then row sums. \(\frac16=\frac4{24}\), \(\frac14=\frac6{24}\), \(\frac18=\frac3{24}\). Row \(X=0\): \(\frac{4+6+3}{24}=\frac{13}{24}\). Row \(X=1\): \(\frac{3+4+4}{24}=\frac{11}{24}\). Check: \(13+11=24\) ✓ (box 1A's normalisation: the whole thing adds to 1).

Sheet step 3 · kill the impossible options. The table has only \(X=0\) and \(X=1\) — the marginal's domain is those two values ("0 otherwise"). (c) and (d) invent \(X=2\) → out. (a)'s fractions don't sum to 1 (\(\frac{7+5}{24}=\frac12\)) → out.

Answer

(b) \(p_X(0)=13/24\), \(p_X(1)=11/24\), 0 otherwise.

6 · The MC words (April 2025 · 1b and friends) — box 1E#

Final exam, 13 April 2025 · 1b · 0.25 p (type)

Which statement about the expected value is true? (The expected value is … the weighted average of the values weighted by the PMF / the simple average of the PMF values / …)

In the cheat sheet: box 1E, first bullet

Sheet step 1 · the printed sentence. "\(E[x]\) … = the weighted average of the outcomes (the probabilities are the weights) = the long-run mean." → the option saying "weighted average of its values, weighted by the PMF" is true (Apr25-1b answer (d)).

Sheet step 2 · the printed traps. ""Integral/sum of the pmf itself" \(=1\), not \(E\). "highest probability / most common" = the mode." → "the simple average of the PMF values" is false; "most likely value" questions ask for the mode, not \(E\).

Answer

The weighted-average statement is true; "simple average of the PMF" is false. Variance: \(\text{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2\); independent \(\Rightarrow\) zero covariance, but zero covariance \(\not\Rightarrow\) independent.

Traps#

Where points are lost (each one is a line of the sheet you skipped)

  • No drawing → wrong limits. The sheet's first sentence: the limits come "from that picture, never from the formula".
  • Forgetting the domain. Bullet 2: "write it and '0 otherwise'". "Indicate its domain" is half the points (Apr24, Jun26).
  • Swapping the roles in the conditional: it is joint / marginal of the GIVEN letter, and the \(x\)-range is "the slice at that \(y\)" (\(y\le x\le1\)), not \(0\le y\le1\).
  • Calling a triangle independent. Bullet 5: "A triangle region → never independent" — on a triangle, knowing \(y\) changes the range of \(x\).
  • Writing \(\frac{c(1-y)^2}{2}+(1-y)\) and panicking because \(y\) is still in it. The sheet: "The answer still holding \(y\) is normal."
  • Forgetting to expand \((1-y)^2\) before integrating — the sheet's rule: "expand powers first".
  • Treating a PMF table like a PDF (or vice versa): table = sum the row, formula on a region = integrate the slice. Same bullet, discrete twin.
  • Letting \(\infty\) into an answer: the sheet's line "Limits can be \(\infty\) (Jun24): \(e^{-\infty}=0\)" — the boundary term always kills the \(e^{-\infty}\) piece.
